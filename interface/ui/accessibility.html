Accessibility

BrandPulse is meant to be usable by as many people as possible: by keyboard alone, with a screen reader, zoomed to 400%, on a phone held either way, without motion, in either display, and printed. This page says how far that is true, how it was checked, and what is not done.

Where it stands

The target is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA. It partially conforms: the automated checks below pass on every page in both displays, and the parts that are not met are listed further down rather than left out.

What you can change here

  • Reduce motion, in the bar at the top of every page, stops everything that moves on its own. Your system’s own setting is always honoured.
  • Dark or Light, beside it.
  • Read as one page, in any report’s chapter bar: the whole report down one column, for a screen reader, for zooming, or for printing.
  • Show as a list, under the Library’s shelf, which is also what a phone, a small window and reduced motion get.
  • Zoom to 400%, or set your own text spacing: nothing should be cut off or need scrolling sideways.

How this was checked

  • In a real browser, on every page, in both displays, at thirteen window sizes from a small phone to an ultrawide screen: the axe-core scanner (WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules), keyboard reach and visible focus, reflow, text spacing, target size, contrast, reduced motion, forced colours, print, and simulated colour blindness, blur and low contrast (ui_evidence/verify_ui.py).
  • In the code, by tests that fail if any of these fixes is undone, each one shown to fail when its fix is deliberately broken.
  • With people: two studies with eight readers in all, one of whom read on a phone and reported low vision. Neither study tested a keyboard, a screen reader or zoom with a person using them.

What does not work yet

  • No disabled person has tested this. The checks above show it can be operated; they cannot show it is comfortable to use every day.
  • It has been checked with VoiceOver on a Mac only by its developer, and not at all with NVDA or JAWS on Windows.
  • Some labels are set at 11.5 pixels, the smallest size the design allows. Zoom, or Read as one page, enlarges them.
  • The charts are pictures with a written summary; the summary, the tables and the text say everything the charts show.
  • Everything it reads is assumed to be in English.
  • Turning a book’s pages, only the title spread carries the report’s main heading; Read as one page gives the whole outline at once.

Who the reports are about

Every report is about a person who never agreed to be read: the reviewer in the video, whose face, voice and words are scored, and the people whose comments make up most of Brand health. None of them was asked, and in this build none of them can contest a reading. Each report carries a page written to the person in the video saying so. A deployed version would owe them a named person to contact, the right to see what a reading was drawn from, a way to have a reading struck, and a say, before release, from disabled readers and from the people it scores.

Telling us about a problem

This is a final-year project that runs on one machine, with no service behind it and no one on call, so there is nowhere to send a report yet. That is itself one of the gaps above, and the report for this project says so.

BrandPulse AI

BrandPulse reads one YouTube video four separate ways: the words that were said, the face on camera, the voice, and what the audience wrote underneath. It scores the moments where those four readings contradict each other. The disagreement is the signal.

Every model runs on this machine. The controller is a local llama3.1:8b served by Ollama on localhost:11434. No pipeline data is sent to a hosted API.

Where it is weak

Two of the four channels are barely better than guessing, and this is stated here rather than buried. On held-out speakers the facial-emotion channel scores 34.2% against an always-neutral constant that scores 67.5%; nine emotion models were benchmarked and none beat the constant, so none was adopted. The vocal channel reaches 48.9%. Both figures were measured on this project’s own hand-labelled data and are reproduced in facial_bench/v2/ and vocal_bench/.

Facial emotion recognition also has documented accuracy disparities across skin tone and gender (Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018; Fan et al., 2023). Every report carries that caveat where its scores are shown.

The four channels

  • Transcript sentiment — what was said.
  • Facial emotion — the face on camera, pooled per segment.
  • Vocal prosody — how it was said, read on arousal.
  • Audience comments — one reading for the whole video.

# Participant information and consent — BrandPulse AI user evaluation

Adapted from the Goldsmiths participant information and consent template supplied as
`Sample-Consent-Forms.docx` with the CM3070 module material. This version was prepared on
12 September 2026 for subsequent evaluation sessions. Participants receive Part 1 before
completing Part 2. Completed forms are stored separately from the public repository.

---

## Part 1 — Information sheet

### What this is

BrandPulse AI is an undergraduate final-year project (CM3070, BSc Computer Science). It
analyses a single YouTube video four separate ways — the words spoken, the face on
camera, the voice, and the audience comments — and scores the moments where those four
readings disagree with each other.

I am asking you to use the interface and tell me what you make of it. **I am evaluating
the interface, not you.** There are no right answers, and "I don't know what that means"
is the single most useful thing you can say.

### What you will be asked to do

One session, about 25–35 minutes, on my laptop, with the system running locally. You will
be asked to read a report the system has already produced, do a few small tasks with it,
and answer questions about what you understood. Some tasks are done with the keyboard
only. You can stop any task, or the whole session, at any point.

### Taking part is voluntary

It is entirely up to you. Saying no costs you nothing and I will not ask again. If you
are a friend or classmate, please take that seriously — there is no obligation here, and
declining will not be awkward.

### You can withdraw

You can withdraw at any time, during the session or afterwards, without giving a reason.
If you withdraw, tell me what you want done with anything you have already said and I
will do it — including deleting it entirely.

**The cut-off is the submission date, 28 September 2026.** After the report is submitted
I cannot remove your data from it. If you want anything removed, tell me before then.

### What is recorded

- Your written or typed answers to the questions.
- Notes I take about what you did — which tasks you completed, where you got stuck,
  what you said out loud while working.
- Whether each task succeeded, and roughly how long it took.

**Not recorded:** audio, video, screen recordings, your face, or anything about the
device you use. No account is created and nothing is uploaded anywhere. The system runs
entirely on my laptop and sends nothing to any external service.

### How you are identified in the report

**Pick one, in Part 2:**

- **Pseudonym (recommended).** You appear as "Participant 1", "Participant 2" and so on.
  Your name appears nowhere in the report, the code, or this repository.
- **First name.** Your first name appears beside your answers.

If you do not express a preference, I will use a pseudonym.

### Where your answers go

Into the project's evaluation chapter and its appendix, quoted verbatim, and into the
project's own working notes. The report is read by markers at the University of London
and Singapore Institute of Management. It is not published, sold, or shared beyond that.

### Storage and retention

Your answers are kept in a plain text file on my laptop until **31 December 2026**, and
deleted after that. The signed consent form is kept separately from the answers, so the
two are not held together.

### Risks

Low. The main one is ordinary awkwardness: you may be shown an interface that turns out
to be confusing, and you may feel that reflects on you. It does not — that is the finding
I am looking for.

If you are uncomfortable with anything, say so and we stop.

### Who to contact

The researcher is the student named on the submitted report. If you have a concern you do
not want to raise with me, raise it with the module's supervisor or with SIM's programme
office.

## August 2026 pilot consent record

The August pilot preceded this written consent process. The record below documents the
procedure used and its limitations.

**Three participants took part on 02 August 2026 with no consent form, no information
sheet, and no recorded discussion of withdrawal, retention or identification.** The
agreement was verbal and informal. The formal consent template was available at the time
but was not used.

The position recorded on 12 September 2026 was:

| | Status |
|---|---|
| Information sheet given | **No** |
| Consent recorded | **No** |
| Withdrawal terms stated | **No** |
| Retention period agreed | **No** |
| Identification preference asked | **No** at the time — asked and answered 12 Sep 2026, below |
| Their answers | Recorded verbatim in a private local file; aggregate findings remain in the repository |
| Their **first names** | Pseudonymised as Participant A, B and C in repository materials on 27 Sep 2026 |
| Their first names in the **submitted report** | **Yes** — in the August draft (body text, appendix headings A.1, A.2, A.3) and in the September report (Appendix A). The report is not in this repository |

### Retrospective confirmation, 12 September 2026

All three participants subsequently confirmed verbally that their ratings could be used
and that they were content to be named in the submitted report. No written confirmation
was collected. This is therefore a retrospective verbal confirmation reported by the
researcher, rather than a participant-produced consent record.

### Update, 27 September 2026: pseudonymised for the public repository

Before public release, the three participants were pseudonymised as **Participant A,
Participant B and Participant C** throughout the repository. Their names remain only in
`user_pilot/participants.local.txt`, which is excluded from version control. Individual
response files from both evaluations are also excluded from the public repository; only
the instruments, aggregate scores and findings are published.

### Remaining limitation

The August pilot did not use a written information sheet or consent form. Permission was
informal and later confirmed verbally, without a dated written response from each
participant. Repository documentation and any academic reporting must describe the process
in those terms and must not imply that written consent was obtained.
